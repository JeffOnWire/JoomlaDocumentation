Separate from any images embedded in an article's text, every article has two optional images of its own: an intro image, shown when the article appears in a listing like a category blog page, and a full article image, shown at the top of the article itself. The two are independent — an article can use the same image for both, or something different for each.

In this tutorial we'll add two more articles to the Astronomy category from 4.1, view them together on a category blog page, then set intro and full article images across all three — using different images on one article to see the two settings work independently, and the same image on the other two, which is the more common approach.

Prerequisites

  • 1 Tutorial Introduction covers where to run your tutorial and administrative access.
  • 4.1 Images in Articles creates the Astronomy category and the Crab Nebula article this tutorial builds on.
  • 2.3 Category Menu Item creates the kind of menu item — Category Blog — we'll reuse here.
  • We'll use these three images in the tutorial, sized for the web at 720px wide. Right-click each and save it to your computer.

Steps

Add Two More Articles

Crab Nebula is currently alone in the Astronomy category. A category blog page with just one article doesn't show much, so let's add two more.

  1. In the Administrator Menu, click Content > Articles.
  2. Click New. Result: A new-article form appears.
    • Title: Jupiter
    • Category: Astronomy
    • Article Text: The largest planet in our solar system, Jupiter is a gas giant more than twice as massive as all the other planets combined.
  3. Click Save & New.
    • Title: Andromeda Galaxy
    • Category: Astronomy
    • Article Text: The Andromeda Galaxy is the nearest large galaxy to the Milky Way, and on a dark, clear night it's visible to the naked eye.
  4. Click Save & Close. Result: The Astronomy category now holds three articles: Crab Nebula, Jupiter, and Andromeda Galaxy.

Create a Category Blog

As demonstrated in 2.3 Category Menu Item, create a menu item to view the category.

  1. In the Administrator Menu, click Menus > Main Menu, then click New.
    • Title: Astronomy Articles Blog
    • Menu Item Type: Category Blog
    • Category: Astronomy
  2. Click Save & Close, leaving all other options at their defaults.
  3. View the site and click the Astronomy Articles Blog menu item. Result: All three articles are listed, but none has an image yet.

Set Different Images on Crab Nebula

Crab Nebula already has an image embedded in its text from 4.1. Here we'll give it its own intro and full article images too — and deliberately use two different photos, so you can see the two settings are independent of each other and of the article's body content.

  1. In the Administrator Menu, click Content > Articles, then click Crab Nebula to open it for editing.
  2. Click the Images and Links tab.
  3. Under Full Article Image, click Select. Result: A popup shows the images already uploaded to the site.
  4. Select the Crab Nebula image already in the Astronomy folder (the same one embedded in the article's text), then click Select in the popup. Result: The image is added as the Full Article Image, with the alt text already carried over.
  5. Under Intro Image, click Select, then Upload the Crab Nebula composite image you downloaded earlier — a different photo of the same nebula, distinct from the close-up used everywhere else in this article.
    • Add alternate text, Crab Nebula composite image.
  6. Click Save & Close.
  7. View the site and return to the Astronomy Articles Blog page. Result: Crab Nebula now shows the composite image in the listing.
  8. Click into the Crab Nebula article. Result: The article page shows a different image at the top — the close-up Full Article Image — followed further down by the same close-up again, embedded in the body text back in 4.1.

Set Matching Images on Jupiter and Andromeda Galaxy

Using two different images, as we just did, is useful when you want a simpler preview image on a listing page and something more detailed on the article itself. Most of the time, though, the same image works fine for both — as we'll do for the remaining two articles.

  1. Open Jupiter for editing, then click the Images and Links tab.
  2. Under Intro Image, click Select, then Upload the Jupiter image you downloaded earlier.
    • Add alternate text, Jupiter.
  3. Under Full Article Image, click Select. Result: The Jupiter image you just uploaded is already there.
  4. Select it and click Select in the popup, rather than uploading it a second time. Result: The same image is now set as both the Intro Image and the Full Article Image.
  5. Click Save & Close.
  6. Open Andromeda Galaxy for editing, then repeat the same steps: upload the Andromeda Galaxy image you downloaded earlier as the Intro Image, then select that same image again as the Full Article Image.
    • Add alternate text, Andromeda Galaxy.
    • Note: If an image is purely decorative and doesn't need to be read aloud by screen readers, you can check No description instead of entering alt text. Since this image is the article's main subject, we've given it real alt text instead.
  7. Click Save & Close.
  8. View the site and return to the Astronomy Articles Blog page. Result: All three articles now show an image in the listing. Click into Jupiter or Andromeda Galaxy and the same image appears again at the top — unlike Crab Nebula, where the listing and the article show two different photos.

Float the Intro Image

An intro image can optionally float beside the article's summary text on a category blog page instead of sitting above it.

  1. Open Jupiter for editing, then click the Images and Links tab.
  2. Look for a Float option near the Intro Image and set it to Left or Right. Note: The exact location of this option can vary by Joomla version and template.
  3. Click Save & Close.
  4. View the site and return to the Astronomy Articles Blog page. Result: The Jupiter image now sits beside its summary text instead of stacked above it, with the text wrapping around it.

Concepts

The intro image and full article image are independent settings. They're commonly set to the same image for simplicity, but using a different image for each can be useful — for example, a simple thumbnail on a listing page and a more detailed image on the article itself.

A category blog page (and similar listings) shows an article's intro image. The article's own page shows the full article image, along with anything embedded directly in the article's text.

As with embedded images, alternate text matters for accessibility. Use No description only for images that are purely decorative and add no meaningful information; see the WebAIM Alternative Text article referenced in 4.1 for more.
