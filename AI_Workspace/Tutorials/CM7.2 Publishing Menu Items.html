Menu Items provide access to articles, not unlike picture frames display artworks that are otherwise stored in the backroom of a museum. This tutorial demonstrates the use of menus and explores the relationship between menus, articles and categories. 

Prerequisites

  • 1 Tutorial Introduction covers where to run your tutorial and administrative access.

Setup

Create a New Category

  1. Log in to the Joomla backend.
  2. In the Administrator Menu, expand Content and click on Categories.
  3. Click on the New button. A new-category form appears.
    • Title: History.
    • Status: Published (default).
  4. Save & Close

Create Two Articles

  1. In the Administrator Menu, expand Content and click on Articles.
  2. Click on the New button. A new-article form appears.
    • Title: The Roman Empire
    • Category: History (default).
  3. Save & Add New
    • Title: Medieval Europe
    • Category: History
  4. Save & Close

Create Menu Items

  1. In the Administrator Menu, click on Menus then Main Menu.
  2. Click on the New button. A new-menu-item form appears.
    • Title: The Romans
    • Menu Item Type: Single Article (Articles > Single Article)
    • Article: The Roman Empire (default).
  3. Save & New
    • Title: History Blog
    • Menu Item Type: Category Blog (Articles > Category Blog)
    • Category: History.
  4. Save & Close

Steps

Verify the Setup

  1. View the frontend.
    • The Main Menu contains links for The Romans and History Blog.
  2. Click on The Romans.
    • The Roman Empire article appears.
  3. Click on History Blog.
    • Both articles appear

Unpublishing Menu Item

  1. In the Administrator Menu, expand Menus and click on Main Menu
  2. Click on the status icon for History Blog and The Romans, changing their status to Unpublished. 
  3. View the website and observe these menu item no longer appear.
    • Our articles are inaccessible.  
    • Menu items must be published to appear in the website.
    • Articles need published menu items.
  4. Republish the menu items.

Effect of Unpublished Articles on Menu Items

  1. In the backend Administrator Menu, view the article list (Content > Articles).
  2. Unpublish The Roman Empire and History Blog by clicking on their status icons.
  3. View the website and observe that both menu items still appear in the main menu. 
  4. Click on The Romans.
    • An error message is displayed.
    • This single-article menu item is still active, but the unpublished article is inaccessible.
  5. Click on History Blog.
    • An empty list appears.
    • The category-blog menu item is active, but will only display its published articles.
  6. Return to the articles list and republish both articles.

Effect of Unpublishing a Category on Menu Items

  1. In the backend Administrator Menu, view the article list (Content > Categories).
  2. Unpublish the History category by clicking on its status icon.
  3. View the website and observe that both menu items still appear in the main menu. 
  4. Click on The Romans.
    • An error message is displayed.
    • This single-article menu item is still active, but the unpublished article is inaccessible because its category is unpublished.
  5. Click on History Blog.
    • An error message is displayed.
    • The category-blog menu item is active, but it requires a published category to function.
  6. Return to the articles list and republish the History category.

Effect of Unpublishing Menu Items

  1. In the backend Administrator Menu, view the article list (Menus > Main Menu).
  2. Unpublish our two menu items, The Romans and History Blog by clicking on their status icons.
  3. View the website and observe the menu items are no longer visible. 
  4. Return to the articles list and republish the menu items.

Concepts

  • Menu Items, Articles, and Categories are interdependent. Menu Items provide access to Articles; Articles and some Menu Items require Categories. 
  • Menu Items can be thought of as the picture frames in which Articles, and lists of Articles (Featured Items, Blogs, etc.) appear. 
  • Menu Item can appear even when the lists or articles they link to have issues.
