Menu items provide access to articles, not unlike picture frames display artworks that are otherwise stored in the backroom of a museum. This tutorial demonstrates the use of menus and explores the relationship between menus, articles, and categories.

Prerequisites

  • 1 Tutorial Introduction covers where to run your tutorial and administrative access.

Setup

Create a New Category

  1. Log in to Joomla backend and navigate to the Home Dashboard.
  2. In the Administrator Menu, click Content > Categories.
  3. Click New. Result: A new-category form appears.
    • Title: History
    • Status: Published (default)
  4. Save & Close

Create Two Articles

  1. In the Administrator Menu, click Content > Articles.
  2. Click New. Result: A new-article form appears.
    • Title: The Roman Empire
    • Category: History (default)
  3. Click Save & New.
    • Title: Medieval Europe
    • Category: History
  4. Save & Close

Create Menu Items

  1. In the Administrator Menu, click Menus > Main Menu.
  2. Click New. Result: A new-menu-item form appears.
    • Title: The Romans
    • Menu Item Type: Articles > Single Article
    • Article: The Roman Empire (default)
  3. Click Save & New.
    • Title: History Blog
    • Menu Item Type: Articles > Category Blog
    • Category: History
  4. Save & Close

Steps

Verify the Setup

  1. View the site. Result: The Main Menu contains links for The Romans and History Blog.
  2. Click The Romans. Result: The The Roman Empire article appears.
  3. Click History Blog. Result: Both articles appear.

Unpublishing Menu Items

  1. In the Administrator Menu, click Menus > Main Menu.
  2. Click the status icon for History Blog and The Romans, changing their status to Unpublished.
  3. View the website. Result: The menu items no longer appear. Note: Menu items must be published to appear on the website, and articles need a published menu item to be accessible.
  4. Republish the menu items.

Effect of Unpublished Articles on Menu Items

  1. In the Administrator Menu, click Content > Articles.
  2. Unpublish The Roman Empire and Medieval Europe by clicking their status icons.
  3. View the website. Result: Both menu items still appear in the Main Menu.
  4. Click The Romans. Result: An error message is displayed. Note: This single-article menu item is still active, but the unpublished article is inaccessible.
  5. Click History Blog. Result: An empty list appears. Note: The category-blog menu item is active, but will only display its published articles.
  6. Return to the articles list and republish both articles.

Effect of Unpublishing a Category on Menu Items

  1. In the Administrator Menu, click Content > Categories.
  2. Unpublish the History category by clicking its status icon.
  3. View the website. Result: Both menu items still appear in the Main Menu.
  4. Click The Romans. Result: An error message is displayed. Note: This single-article menu item is still active, but the article is inaccessible because its category is unpublished.
  5. Click History Blog. Result: An error message is displayed. Note: The category-blog menu item is active, but it requires a published category to function.
  6. Return to the category list and republish the History category.

Effect of Unpublishing Menu Items

  1. In the Administrator Menu, click Menus > Main Menu.
  2. Unpublish the two menu items, The Romans and History Blog, by clicking their status icons.
  3. View the website. Result: The menu items are no longer visible.
  4. Return to the menu list and republish the menu items.

Concepts

Menu items, articles, and categories are interdependent. Menu items provide access to articles; articles and some menu items require categories.

Menu items can be thought of as the picture frames in which articles, and lists of articles (featured items, blogs, etc.), appear.

Menu items can appear even when the lists or articles they link to have issues.
